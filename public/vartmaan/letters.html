Letters to the editor

Illustrative letters: common questions from newsroom desks, written by us, not from customers.

The questions desks ask most about Vartmaan AI, each answered by the editor.

This week's letters

We already file on WhatsApp. Do our reporters have to change?

Sir,

Our reporters file on WhatsApp, a few by email, and the younger ones use an app. Will this make them learn something new?

Bureau chief, Udaipur

The editor replies

No. Vartmaan takes stories from WhatsApp, Outlook and the Reporter app into one inbox. Reporters keep filing the way they do now, and keep typing their slug in the usual pattern, DDMMYY_CITY_SUBJECT_TYPE_REPORTER. Vartmaan keeps it as typed.

Can a reporter file a story through the FTP?

Sir,

Our video team lives on the FTP. Could the story go there too, with the footage?

Video desk, Jaipur

The editor replies

No. The FTP is not a filing channel. Reporters upload footage there and Vartmaan finds it by the slug; the story itself comes in on WhatsApp, in Outlook or through the Reporter app.

Uploads are capped at 500 MB, so larger files go by FTP. The Reporter app takes up to 20 files in one report.

Will it touch our footage folders?

Sir,

Our folders are named the way the video desk likes them. I do not want software moving things around.

FTP administrator, Jaipur

The editor replies

It will not. Vartmaan never changes the reporter's footage; the only place it writes is its own folder. The desk can rename or move Vartmaan's own story folders, and nothing else. It reads the district's folder whatever its date folders are called, and checks day folders one day either side.

A photo, then text, then a voice note. Do we get three items?

Sir,

Our reporters send a story in pieces, minutes apart. The desk loses track.

Assignment desk, Kota

The editor replies

One item. Texts, photos and voice notes sent within 90 seconds become a single item. Each item is sorted as a story, press release, day plan or not a story, and the inbox shows stories only unless you ask for the rest.

What happens when the footage is late?

Sir,

Visuals often reach the FTP an hour after the text. Does the story sit there blind?

Assignment editor, Jodhpur

The editor replies

The story shows Waiting. Vartmaan scans the FTP every 5 minutes and keeps checking for up to 4 hours; after that the story shows Not found.

At any point the desk can press Re-check, or Pick folder to choose the folder by hand; the story then shows Picked by hand.

Does Vartmaan write the story for us?

Sir,

I have heard these tools draft copy. Our desk would rather write its own.

Chief sub-editor, Jaipur

The editor replies

No. The text is the reporter's and the edits are yours, made in the story editor. AI suggestions are planned, and they are not live.

How does an approved story reach Octopus?

Sir,

And can we tell whether it aired?

Output desk, Jaipur

The editor replies

Approve waits until the files are on the FTP, then the story enters the feed for Octopus with a Hindi byline, as a FLASH, an UPDATE or a KILL. The progress line shows each step: Received, Media, Review, FTP folder, RSS feed.

Reading back from Octopus, to show whether a story aired and in which bulletin, is coming next. It is not live yet.

Who on the desk can approve a story?

Sir,

We have stringers, desk editors and a chief editor who only wants to watch.

Desk administrator, Bikaner

The editor replies

That depends on the role. Vartmaan has 5 roles built from 15 permission keys; a chief editor's role, for example, can see the overview and the intake without changing anything.

Ten failed logins within 15 minutes lock the account. Switching single pages or actions for one person is planned, not live.
